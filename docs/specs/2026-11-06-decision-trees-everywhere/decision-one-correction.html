Why K002 failed: two of your rulings get the same answers

K002 asks three questions about a section of a general skill:

  1. Does it contain one product's own material: its commands, files, steps or state, including in a worked example or a quote?
  2. Is all of that material labelled as an example?
  3. Does it quote a real product's own mistake?

It warns in two cases: product material that isn't all labelled as examples, or a quoted real product mistake.

It ran on 29 development cases:

  • 12 right;
  • 12 unsure, 9 of them only because the kind filter was unsure first;
  • 5 false warnings.

No real mistake got a pass, but that doesn't mean every real mistake warned: some of the unsure cases were real mistakes. Three of the false warnings are cases you ruled quiet in U16 row 7: using a public tool through its documented interface. Here are two real cases you ruled on:

Our working explanation is that question 1 asks whether the section names commands and files, while your boundary is whose commands they are and how they're used. The rule's reference already states your row-7 boundary, but question 1 doesn't ask about it. That explanation is a hypothesis to test, not proven.

Why the kind filter failed: the errors are clear, the cause isn't yet

It sorted 65 development folders: 53 right, 6 unsure, 6 wrong. The wrong ones were confident (.73–.98).

  • Four mixed up general and domain. For example, you ruled decision-model-review a domain skill (a subject's standards); the filter said general (a workflow), at .78. Blind labellers called principle-types-are-communication general; the filter said domain, at .73. K002 runs on both kinds, so these four don't change its results, but they're still wrong.
  • Two are one of your rulings. You ruled practices-verification-maintenance repo-specific; the filter said general (a reusable practice), at .98, and was scored on it twice. Your ruling stands. What we don't know yet is why it disagreed: either the text the filter reads doesn't show what makes the skill repo-specific, or our definition of repo-specific is too narrow. Diagnosing that comes first, and it decides which one gets fixed.
  • Some examples were also scored cases. The independent Reviewer found that two of the filter's worked examples are the descriptions of skills it was then scored on, with their answers attached. One is the domain-decision-models description, labelled domain; the other is Agent Studio's, labelled tool manual. Our own standard says an example never comes from the piece being judged. Nobody claims it changed the answers, but those cases can't show the filter works on text it hasn't seen. A corrected filter must keep its examples and its cases apart.

One narrower setting exists: with a confidence cutoff of .9, the filter labelled no folder wrongly, but 31 of 65 came back unsure and would be skipped. Your requirements allow skipping, but it leaves about half the skills unread and doesn't fix K002.

The choice

Conditions 1 to 3 are the development ones: enough real cases, cutoffs set on development, and on the frozen development cases every real mistake warns and every good text passes. Condition 4 is the held-out run. K002's held-out cases are all passages that should stay quiet, so that run checks false warnings, not whether new mistakes get caught. Condition 5 is that the engine leaves no result incomplete.

Pick one, and use Comments if you want to qualify it. If you don't answer, PR 2 still comes to you to merge, both rules stay off, and no further work on these two rules starts.
